Optical Transfer

Move a file or text snippet from one screen to another device’s camera as an endless fountain-coded QR stream. No payload upload, network path, account, pairing, app, key, tracking, or relay.

Not confidential: any camera that can see the sending screen can recover the payload. Optical Transfer removes the network path; it does not encrypt the screen.

Prepare payload

Everything is packed locally. Payloads are limited to 16 MB, but camera, decoder, compression, and recovery buffers make peak memory higher; text snippets are limited to 1 MB.

Choose a file or enter text, then prepare it.
Transfer tuning

The safe default is 1,465 bytes at 24 FPS. Denser codes or 60 FPS may reduce real throughput when autofocus or display refresh causes more drops.

Sending screen

Turn up brightness, keep the whole QR code visible, and hold or prop the receiving camera steady. The animated high-contrast pattern may affect people sensitive to flashing; Pause freezes it, and the stream is limited to 2 FPS without catch-up bursts when your device requests reduced motion.

Receive tuning

One worker is the memory-safe default; each worker instantiates its own embedded ZXing WASM decoder. Busy workers drop frames instead of queueing stale video—the fountain code tolerates that.

Protocol and security boundaries

Each DCF2 container carries a safe filename, media type, exact compressed and original lengths, and SHA-256. A 20-byte frame header identifies the stream. Frames are LT fountain equations selected by a deterministic robust-soliton distribution; two distinct valid frames are required before replacing an active session. Completion stays below 100% and no download exists until both the optical checksum and SHA-256 pass.
